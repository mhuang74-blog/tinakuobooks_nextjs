# Work pages editorial plan

A work page should give someone something worth reading while helping them decide whether to share, teach, or enquire about a story. A parent should be able to get a feel for its tone. A teacher should find a discussion idea they can use without asking children to share private experiences. An editor or rights agent should be able to identify the work and its contributors and find the contact form.

## Write from a different point of view

Use the works catalogue to check names, story events, and publication details. Start the writing with something specific to the book that the catalogue entry does not already do, such as the gap between what Ding Xiaofei writes and what he lets his classmates see. Keep the setup short, leave discoveries for the reader, and write about how a story works or what it makes room to notice. Use a warm, observant voice and let the humour come from the scene. Keep personal memories, reader reactions, and creative intentions out of the copy unless the author has supplied them. The site remains a portfolio maintained by Michael Huang, who also wrote the colouring post.

Write the Traditional Chinese copy for readers in Taiwan. Develop the English text from the same editorial angle in idiomatic English. Keep titles and names consistent across the site, including the names of collaborators, and identify English catalogue titles as working translations where their status is provisional.

## Make the surface match the story

- Give each work one short hook that can serve the shelf, search results, and sharing cards. Write a separate opening for the pop-up that stands on its own. Give the full page more room to develop the story, point out a narrative choice, or suggest something to try while reading.
- Use reading format and age guidance from the catalogue. Give early chapter books their own shelf so families can find them. Keep the established links for existing collections.
- Offer a specific reading or discussion prompt when it fits the work. Flag themes such as animal harm, parental separation, and death where knowing about them helps an adult choose a book.
- Keep reading guidance and creative credits compact. Use the catalogue for edition details and distinguish English introductions from the languages in which the work appeared.
- Scope the site's licence language to its original editorial text. Direct permissions and rights enquiries to the contact form without making promises about availability.

## Check before publishing

Review the two languages side by side against the catalogue and the old copy. Search for long verbatim matches, outdated descriptions, publisher quotes, unsupported claims, and spoilers. Compare the amount of text in each full post with the original and read the pop-up on its own. Check the first-time reader's path from shelf to work page in both languages. Cover a bilingual print book, a contribution to an anthology, a stage credit, and the colouring download in the verification.
