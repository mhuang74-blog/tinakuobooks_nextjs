In “The Mysterious Spiral Bread,” Hu Abu leaves his old line of work for a bakery. Children who read the first collection will recognise the fox, but the bakery is a perfectly good place to meet him for the first time.

An anthology lets readers come in through something they already know. Children who watch livestreams may pay attention to what the camera can see. Those who have read Li Bai may want to know what a line they recognise is doing in a mystery. “The Mysterious Spiral Bread” offers a different way in. Here the detective is the person learning a new job, and everyday work can take him somewhere his old routine wouldn’t.

If you’re reading in a group, there’s no need to get everyone to the same page before they have something to share. Ask each reader to jot down what they thought the story was going to be about after the first few pages. After they finish, they can tell the group which detail sent them back to look again. They can do it without saying who did it.

There are seven stories, each of which stands on its own. One group in class might start with the school livestream, another with the clues in Li Bai’s poetry. Afterwards, compare the moments that made each group rethink its first guess.
