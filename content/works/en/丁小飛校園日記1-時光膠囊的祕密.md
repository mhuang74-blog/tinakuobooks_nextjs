The fourth graders are putting diaries in a time capsule that won’t be opened for fifty years. For Ding Xiaofei, it’s a place to put something he can’t say at school. The girl he likes admires somebody else, and he has no intention of waiting fifty years to do something about it.

A smiley face on a reading assignment is easy to glance at and move on from. Xiaofei remembers the one Cheng Youshen draws for him in much more detail than that. The funny part comes when he tries to think about what she might like and fills his head up with his own big plans instead. The deputy class president, Jin Sigao, is around to make matters worse. A few small things at school become major diary events as far as Xiaofei is concerned.

A time capsule gives those small events a surprisingly long life. If someone really did open the diary in fifty years, what would catch their attention? Maybe it would be the smiley face. Or it might be the restless feeling Xiaofei has in class, familiar to a child reading today and very easy to forget once you grow up. Try picking one ordinary classroom moment from the diary that you’d save for someone in the future.

In this first book, much of the comedy is in the gap between Ding Xiaofei’s account and what’s going on around him. Pick an entry and imagine how the classmate at the next desk would have written up the same day.
