To Ding Xiaofei, becoming a class officer is the first step towards doing something important. He’s even worked out which job he has a shot at. Then He Li-Luo is nominated. His rival is also his close friend, and they still have to see each other every day.

Xiaofei has put so much thought into the election because he’s already tied this year’s class jobs to the future he pictures for himself. His grades rule out running for class president. The deputy’s job, on the other hand, is something he can go after. It sounds like a perfectly sensible plan while it’s still written down in his own diary and nobody else has had a say.

Once a friend becomes a rival, a word that would have sounded ordinary the day before can get under your skin. Xiaofei has a knack for coming up with good reasons for what he does. His diary gives us time to notice what he’s working around. Pay attention when he sounds especially sure of himself. Those are often the passages worth reading another time, if only to see how his account changes when you know what he wanted.

The diary lets readers hear Ding Xiaofei’s side first. Try writing the same election as an entry in He Li-Luo’s diary. What would he put in, and what might he never think to mention?
