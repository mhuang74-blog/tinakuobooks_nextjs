Bala and Billy have three days to show a robot inspector that their delivery shop is helping people. In walks a girl with a request of her own. Her grandmother is ninety-six, and she’d like to send her some courage.

The customer knows how she hopes her grandmother will feel even though she can’t point to the object she wants sent. A grown-up and a child could each try packing an imaginary box. One might reach for a note and the other for a small object. Give each a chance to explain how their parcel might help before deciding which one to send.

In the first book, the problem was what would happen to a block of ice in transit. Here you might pause at the counter and draw up a shipping label together. Grandma’s name goes on the label. What goes in the box is up to the child.
