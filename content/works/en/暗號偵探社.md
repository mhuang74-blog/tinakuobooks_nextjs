It’s a divorce agreement. Lanya gets Chicken Drumstick Hero and Qiqi to help, and the three skip school to work out what’s happening between his parents and whether they can change it. It’s easy to get caught up in Lanya’s search and start reading every little gesture as a clue.

The thing Lanya is trying to investigate is so close to home that almost anything might look like a sign. A grown-up pauses in the middle of a sentence, and he wonders what ought to have come next. He notices a gesture he knows and tries to remember how it looked last time. That kind of attention can pick up small things others miss. It can also take him further into a guess than the evidence will bear.

Because Chicken Drumstick Hero and Qiqi go along with him, readers get to see how three children can look at the same thing and come away with different thoughts. There’s a difference between helping a friend think through what he could do, giving him time to finish what he wants to say, and asking him to solve something adults have to decide. A reader who has never been in Lanya’s situation still has a way in through the friendship.

With teens, keep the conversation inside the story. Which clues does Lanya actually see? Which are the ones he wants to see? That leaves room to talk about parents separating and what it means for friends to stick around.
