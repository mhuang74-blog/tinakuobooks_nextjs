If you had to turn one thing in the living room into a spaceship, the sofa would do nicely. There’s even room for the dog. A-Ninggu and Dad climb aboard after A-Bai tries to get out of giving a speech at the school celebration and tumbles into a black hole beneath the cushions.

A-Bai’s difficulty before the adventure is an ordinary one. He has been asked to speak in front of the school and would much rather find somewhere to hide. Children in the audience may know what that feels like. The adults may remember waiting for a child to walk onstage. The black hole gives both groups a way to picture the distance that can open up inside a home when somebody is worried and the others are still working out how to reach them.

Luo Yijun’s book began with moments of family life. In the musical those relationships have to carry an adventure that everyone takes part in. Tina Kuo developed the story, and 林孟寰 and 張元 wrote the script. There’s an easy way to try out the same leap from household object to set piece. Use an ordinary chair and see if you can make someone guess the moment it takes off without naming what it’s turned into. Would it take a sound, someone shifting their weight, or the reactions of the people sitting next to it?

The musical draws on Luo Yijun’s Little Son. Onstage, a piece of everyday furniture becomes the way into an adventure. For a young audience, it makes the jump from a living room to outer space easy to picture. Ask children to pick something at home and imagine what it could become onstage.
