A classmate has something to show off, so Ding Xiaofei claims he owns a pair of limited-edition glasses. There’s just one catch: he can’t produce them. Keeping that story going is work enough. Then some prize money from a TV contest goes missing, and people start looking at him.

At first, Xiaofei sees something one of his classmates has and wants to put himself in the same company. Soon the story is out of his hands. How much of the original claim do his classmates remember by the time it comes back to him? How much have they added for themselves? From his notes we get to watch him want very badly to be believed while dreading the next question.

Try keeping two columns as you read. In one, put what Xiaofei says out loud. In the other, put what his classmates decide must have happened. Some things will end up in both, and some won’t. When the prize money disappears, look back to see which details tell you something about the money and which only tell you what the class already thinks of Xiaofei.

There are two things to untangle here. Ding Xiaofei did lie about the glasses. What evidence does anyone have that he took the money? When the class starts guessing, ask children to look for an actual clue.
